Workshop Overview

Radio Foxhunting · ARDF One-Day Workshop

A full-day scouting activity where teams hunt hidden 144 MHz radio transmitters using Yagi antennas, compasses, and topographic maps. Designed for JOTA JOTI 2026 at UMPSA.

Duration
1 Day
~6.5 hours of activities
Frequency
144 MHz
2-meter amateur band
Beacons
5 Foxes
MO MOE MOI MOS MOH
Team Size
2–4
Scouts per team
Age Group
11–17
Secondary scouts
Max Teams
10–12
Per facilitator group
Amateur Radio Licence note: Transmitters must be operated by a licensed amateur radio operator (MCMC B licence minimum) at all times during field deployment. Scouts may use the receive equipment without a licence.
ARDF Format used: Simplified IARU Region 3 rules. 5 foxes on sequential 12-second transmission slots, one minute master oscillator beacon. Teams hunt on foot. Paper punch-card scoring. No finish-line time penalty for this beginner format.
⚠ Site safety: Walk the course before the event. Mark any hazards on your master map. Ensure all scouts carry a whistle (3 blasts = emergency), a water bottle, and know the rally point.

Curriculum

Learning Objectives

Bloom's taxonomy levels noted. Assessable outcomes for each workshop module.

1
Remember · Understand
Scouts can explain the ARDF beacon cycle (5 foxes, 12-second slots, 60-second MO), identify each fox by its Morse identifier, and state the frequency and band used (144 MHz, 2m).
2
Apply
Scouts can take a compass bearing on a signal maximum using a Yagi antenna and plot that bearing line from their current position on a 1:10 000 topographic map.
3
Apply · Analyse
Scouts can triangulate a transmitter location from two or more bearing lines, select the best approach route given terrain, and estimate remaining distance from signal strength.
4
Analyse · Evaluate
Scouts can apply the body-null technique and use inline attenuators to close in on a transmitter at short range, adjusting technique as signal strength increases.
5
Create · Evaluate
Teams can independently plan a hunt strategy (fox visit order, pacing, time budget) and adapt the plan in the field when initial triangulation misses, demonstrating computational and critical thinking.
6
Understand · Apply
Scouts can demonstrate safe radio and field practices: no transmission on 144 MHz without a licensed operator present, stay-on-trail rules, emergency whistle signal, and buddy-system check-in procedure.

Assessment

Facilitator Assessment Rubric

Observe teams during field run. Score each criterion 1–4. Total = sum of all criteria × 2.5 = 100 point scale.

Teamwork & Roles Roles clearly assigned; constant communication; adapts strategy Roles mostly observed; some communication breakdowns One or two members dominate; others not engaged No defined roles; team frequently disagrees
Safety & Rules All safety protocols followed throughout; stays within boundaries One minor oversight; corrected without prompting Two or more minor oversights; required facilitator reminder Serious safety breach or deliberate rule violation
Field Strategy Efficiently plans fox visit order; manages time; finds 5 foxes Finds 3–4 foxes within time; adequate time management Finds 1–2 foxes; poor route selection wastes time Finds 0 foxes; no evidence of strategy
CT & Problem Solving Adapts plan on the fly; uses data from misses to revise estimate Makes some adaptations; partially applies decomposition Follows sequence rigidly; does not adapt when plan fails No evidence of systematic approach
Scoring: Sum all 8 criteria (max 32 points). Multiply by 3.125 for 100-point scale. Foxes found is a separate tally: award a certificate to teams finding ≥ 3 foxes. Certificate of Participation to all scouts who complete the full day.

Data Collection

Google Sheets Tracker Setup

The LMS tracker.js pushes progress events to a Google Apps Script endpoint. Set up the sheet before the workshop day.

1
Create the spreadsheet
Open Google Sheets and create a new sheet named RF_Workshop_2026. Create four tabs: progress, survey_pre, survey_post, and scores.
2
Add the Apps Script web app
In the spreadsheet, open Extensions → Apps Script. Paste the tracker endpoint script (available in the workshop facilitator pack). Deploy as a Web App with Execute as: Me and Who has access: Anyone. Copy the deployment URL.
3
Update tracker.js
Open tracker.js and set SHEET_ENDPOINT to your deployment URL. Save and redeploy the LMS. The tracker sends POST requests with { scoutId, module, activity, ts } payloads on each activity completion.
4
Set up the progress tab columns
Required column headers in the progress tab:
timestamp scout_id team_name module activity score duration_s
5
Test before the event
Open a student page on the event device and complete one activity. Within 5 seconds, a row should appear in the progress tab. If not, check the Apps Script execution log for CORS or permission errors. Most issues resolve by re-deploying the web app with a new version.
6
Monitor live during the workshop
Keep the spreadsheet open on the facilitator device. Use conditional formatting on the module column to highlight stuck teams (no new rows after 25 minutes). The scores tab auto-calculates per-team completion percentage from a COUNTIF formula.

Field Wisdom

Facilitator Tips for Scouts

Collected from previous ARDF workshops. Address these proactively in Module 1 to avoid delays in the field.

Signal Confusion
Scouts hear everything at 144.500 MHz
First-time scouts often cannot isolate the fox signal from background noise.
  • Teach them to listen for rhythm, not just volume
  • Have them listen to all 5 fox IDs before the run
  • Remind them MO = – – – is the master oscillator, not a fox
Map Reading
They rotate the map, not themselves
Scouts turn the paper map to face their direction of travel. This reverses east and west on the ground.
  • Drill: hold map flat, align north arrow to compass north — always
  • Practice indoors with a simple floor plan before the field
  • Mark their start point together before they disperse
Antenna Use
They sweep too fast and miss the null
A 3-element Yagi has a front beam width of ~60°. A scout sweeping in 2 seconds misses the peak.
  • Teach: one full 360° rotation should take at least 20 seconds
  • Watch the signal meter, not the landscape
  • Record bearing in notebook before moving — memory fails on rough terrain
Close-In Phase
They get close and then run past the fox
When within 20 m, the signal reaches the receiver from all directions. Scouts think the fox moved.
  • At full-scale deflection, add 20 dB attenuator immediately
  • Switch to body-null: stand still, turn slowly on the spot
  • Fox is always in the null direction (directly behind you when signal drops)
Time Management
Teams chase the farthest fox first
Natural tendency is to go for the most exciting fox. This leaves scouts far from base when time runs out.
  • Brief on score: 10 pts/fox regardless of distance
  • Suggest nearest-fox-first strategy as a starting heuristic
  • Issue a 30-minute and 15-minute return whistle reminder
Teamwork
One scout dominates, others disengage
Often the scout who built the antenna becomes the team's only operator.
  • Enforce role rotation: map reader, antenna operator, recorder rotate each fox
  • Grade on individual participation in the rubric, not just team result
  • In debrief: ask each team member to name one decision they made
Computational Thinking
Help scouts see the algorithm in the hunt
Connect CT vocabulary explicitly during the field debrief.
  • Decomposition: each fox is an independent sub-problem
  • Pattern recognition: each fox has the same 5-step find sequence
  • Algorithm: the close-in procedure is a deterministic search loop
  • Abstraction: the map replaces walking the terrain in advance
Equipment
Antenna connections loosen on rough ground
3-element Yagi boom connections and the coax-to-receiver BNC joint are the most common failure points.
  • Issue electrical tape with each team's kit for field repairs
  • Demonstrate antenna assembly and disassembly at start
  • Have one spare receiver at base camp; swap immediately, don't diagnose in field
