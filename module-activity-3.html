Tier 2 — Debug the Scenario Applied

Identify and fix the error in each scenario.

📋 Scenario 1: A student's driven element is 110 cm for 144 MHz. Calculate the correct length and show the formula.
📋 Scenario 2: A scout reports that signal is strongest when the Yagi points AWAY from the beacon. What technique explains this, and why does it work?
📋 Scenario 3: The spacings are reversed: Director is 36.5 cm from the Driven element, Reflector is 30.5 cm. Describe the correct spacings and explain which element should be closer.
Scenario 1 — Correct driven element:
Formula: λ/2 × vf = (14300 / 144) × 0.97 = 99.31 × 0.97 ≈ 96.3 cm (theoretical). The measured value for the tape-measure design is 99.4 cm to account for tape width end effects. The student's 110 cm is too long — it would resonate lower than 144 MHz, increasing SWR.
Scenario 2 — Body shielding / back-lobe technique:
This is the body shielding technique. When very close to the fox, the signal is so strong that the front lobe saturates. The scout turns their back to the fox so their body absorbs some of the signal. Combined with the weaker back lobe of the Yagi, the null behind the antenna gives a much sharper bearing reading at close range.
Scenario 3 — Correct spacings:
Correct: Reflector → Driven = 36.5 cm (reflector is behind the driven, further back). Driven → Director = 30.5 cm (director is in front, closer). The director is shorter and placed closer to the driven element to increase gain in the forward direction.

Tier 3 — Signal Sweep Direction Finding

Your Yagi antenna is most sensitive in the direction it points. Click the compass to rotate the antenna and watch the signal meter. When the bar peaks — that's the fox direction!

How it works: A Yagi has a strong front lobe (±40°) and near-zero signal from the back. Sweep the antenna slowly around 360° until you find the bearing with maximum signal strength.
