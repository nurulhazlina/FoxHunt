Tier 1 — Fill-in-Blanks Answer Key

Q1. Contour lines that are close together indicate ___ terrain.
✓ steep
Contour interval stays constant; close lines = rapid elevation change = steep ground.
Q2. A compass bearing is measured in ___ from 0 to 360.
✓ degrees
Compass bearings use the full 360° circle, measured clockwise from North.
Q3. The triangulation technique uses at least ___ bearings.
✓ 2 (accept: 2 or 3)
Two lines can intersect at one point. Three lines give a "cocked hat" with a probability zone. Both are valid.
Q4. Easting is read ___ on a grid.
✓ first (accept: left-right, before northing, left to right)
Easting (horizontal/corridor) is always read before Northing (vertical/stairs) in the MGRS system.
Q5. When two bearing lines cross, you have found the ___ position.
✓ fox (accept: fox's position, fox position)
The intersection of two bearing lines is the estimated transmitter (fox) location.
Q6. Magnetic declination is the angle between ___ north and ___ north.
✓ true / magnetic (either order)
True North = geographic pole; Magnetic North = compass needle direction. The angle between them varies by location and year.
Q7. A 6-figure grid reference has ___ digits.
✓ 6
Three digits easting + three digits northing = six digits total, giving ~100 m accuracy.

Tier 2 — Debug Scenario Model Answers

Scenario 1 — Same-position bearings
Problem: Same location produces only one line, not two. Triangulation requires two different positions.
Fix: Move to a second observation point at least 50 m away before taking the second bearing. The two lines must come from different positions to create an intersection. Award marks for: identifying "same position" as the flaw, explaining that two lines are needed, and describing the correct method.
Scenario 2 — Map scale calculation
Scale 1:25 000 → 1 cm = 250 m. Working: 4 cm × 250 m/cm = 1,000 m = 1 km.
Award partial marks for correct method with arithmetic errors. Full marks require showing the working and the unit conversion.
Scenario 3 — Invalid bearing 450°
Error: 450° exceeds the 0–359° range. Correct: 450° − 360° = 090° (due East).
Accept "90°" or "090°". Scouts should recognise that bearings always reduce to the 0–359° range by subtracting 360°.

Tier 3 — Code Answer & Marking Criteria

Model Solution

function triangulate(obs1, bearing1, obs2, bearing2) {
  // Convert bearings (degrees clockwise from N) to radians
  var r1 = bearing1 * Math.PI / 180;
  var r2 = bearing2 * Math.PI / 180;

  // Direction vectors: sin for x (east), -cos for y (north-up on screen)
  var dx1 = Math.sin(r1), dy1 = -Math.cos(r1);
  var dx2 = Math.sin(r2), dy2 = -Math.cos(r2);

  // Solve obs1 + t1*d1 = obs2 + t2*d2 for t1
  var denom = dx1 * dy2 - dy1 * dx2;
  if (Math.abs(denom) < 1e-10) return null; // parallel / same bearing

  var t1 = ((obs2.x - obs1.x) * dy2 - (obs2.y - obs1.y) * dx2) / denom;
  return {
    x: obs1.x + t1 * dx1,
    y: obs1.y + t1 * dy1
  };
}

Common Mistakes & How to Address Them

Confusing True North with Magnetic North

Scouts often treat compass bearing as pointing exactly to geographic north. Fix: Demonstrate with a map that shows the declination diagram. For JOTA-JOTI in Malaysia (~0.7° E), the error is negligible, but the conceptual distinction matters.

Taking bearings from the same position

Scouts record two bearings without moving between positions. Fix: Emphasise: "New position = new bearing." Use the triangulation animation to show why a single point cannot give a fix.

Reversing Easting and Northing

Writing Northing first. Fix: Drill the corridor/stairs mnemonic until automatic. Practice reading 5–6 references aloud before the field session.

Bearing out of range (e.g. recording 400°)

Scouts add increments past 360° without resetting. Fix: Teach: always reduce modulo 360. A bearing of 400° = 040°.

Near-parallel bearing lines

Two bearings that are nearly the same (e.g., 042° and 046°) produce a very uncertain, elongated intersection zone. Fix: Instruct scouts to seek observation points where the angle between lines is close to 90°.

Tips for Running the Field Session

  • Prepare a 1:25 000 or 1:10 000 printout of the foxhunting area with a 100 m grid overlay.
  • Pre-mark 3 observation posts (OP-A, OP-B, OP-C) on the map, at roughly 90° angles to the planned fox position.
  • Have scouts work in pairs: one holds the map, one takes the bearing and calls it out.
  • Require scouts to record time alongside each bearing so you can discuss signal variation over time.
  • After the exercise, compare estimated fox grid references against the actual transmitter location and measure the error.
  • Discuss why teams with more bearings (closer to "cut on 3") tend to have smaller errors.
Timing: Allow 15 min for Module 2 study, 30 min for activities, 20 min for the field demo. Total: ~65 min.
