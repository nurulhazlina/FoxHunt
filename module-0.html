What is Radio Foxhunting?

ARDF
Amateur Radio Direction Finding — the official name for radio foxhunting
Combines
🏃 Orienteering & navigation + 📻 Electronics + 📡 Radio direction finding
Objective
Find 5 hidden transmitters ("foxes") in an 80-minute competitive hunt
Beacon Cycle
Each fox transmits for 12 seconds in a rotating 60-second cycle

▲ Animated map — watch the hunter triangulate toward each beacon

Meet the 5 Beacons

Fox #CallsignMorse PatternTx TimeLocation Hint
Fox 1 MOE − − · (1 dit) 12 sNorthern tree line
Fox 2 MOI − − · · (2 dits) 12 sNear the stream
Fox 3 MOS − − · · · (3 dits) 12 sSouth ridge clearing
Fox 4 MOH − − · · · · (4 dits) 12 sHilltop clearing
Fox 5 MO5 − − · · · · · (5 dits) 12 sEast boundary fence
Rest MO − − (rest beacon) 12 sStart / Finish area
💡 Cycle Tip

The MO rest beacon plays between each fox transmission. Listen for the pattern MO → MOE → MO → MOI → MO → … to synchronise your hunting and predict which fox is next.

Safety Rules

⚠️ Read Before Leaving the Start Area
  • Always hunt with your partner — never alone in the field
  • Carry your water bottle; drink at least 200 mL every 20 minutes
  • Stay within the orange boundary markers at all times
  • If injured: blow the emergency whistle 3 times and stay put
  • Mobile phones are for emergencies only — not for navigation or GPS
  • Closed-toe shoes are mandatory; no sandals, thongs or bare feet
  • Return to Start/Finish before the 80-minute horn sounds (late return = time penalty)
  • Follow all instructions from hunt officials immediately and without argument
